KM 01 · Mirpur Khas district, Sindh · 2016

Five cousins

Five cousins of the Thakor family play hide and seek around their village temple. The youngest, Naresh, slips into the sugarcane field and never comes out. Within weeks two more of the boys are gone.

Of the five who played that morning, only Ramesh walks away whole.

KM 02 · Tharparkar, Sindh · August 2018

Heera, the diamond

A village in the Thar desert celebrates the coming of age of an eleven-year-old girl with large, bright eyes and feet hardened by work.

Months later she is taken from her family. Her father, Bhagchand, a man with no influence, refuses to give up on her.

KM 03 · Islamabad · 2020

A call at the hour of prayer

At the Indian High Commission, a young trainee called Arindham Bose answers a phone he was not supposed to pick up. A woman on the line asks for help.

It concerns a Hindu family from Sindh with a girl child.

KM 04 · On the train to the border · March 2020

The man on the train

A stranger notices how frightened Bhagchand's family looks and offers them water and a place in his group. It is Ramesh. At the last checkpoint he is held back, and blinks once to tell them to go.

It's okay. Go.

KM 05 · Attari border · 11 March 2020

This land is yours

Bhagchand, his wife Rani, Heera and Kavita walk into India. An officer in a bright yellow turban pours water into a tin cup and hands it to the family.

Why so late? This land is yours. Here, you are not guests, you are home.

KM 06 · Amritsar · March 2020

I will sit with that bhaiyya

Kiran has come to meet them. Heera points to the empty seat on his motorcycle and climbs on. On the ride she chants the Hanuman Chalisa aloud for the first time in her life.

At the Golden Temple the family eats at the langar, without fear, as guests and equals.

KM 07 · Delhi · 2020

The escalator at Chawri Bazar

Heera and Kavita see a metro station for the first time. The lift frightens them, so Heera chooses the escalator instead, holding her sister's hand.

Bhaiyya, let's go on the escalator. Take me.

KM 08 · An ashram in Kerala · 5 November 2020

A midnight call to my mother

On her birthday, at midnight, Kiran finally tells his mother about the ride. After a long silence she gives him her blessing, and one condition.

Call me twice a day, and promise me one thing, never ride after dark.

KM 09 · Kanyakumari · November 2020

Where three seas meet

The Yatra begins at Triveni Sangam, the southern tip of India, with a vow to live only on what strangers offer.

KM 10 · Tamil Nadu · November 2020

The Eelam Tamil camps

The first camps on the route are home to Tamil families who fled the war in Sri Lanka. Kiran listens first, and writes everything down.

KM 11 · Hampi and Anegundi, Karnataka · November 2020

Kishkindha

Kiran bows before Virupaksha and rides on to Anegundi, the ancient Kishkindha of the Ramayana, where he is welcomed by the royal family.

KM 12 · Sindhanur, Karnataka · November 2020

Bengal in Karnataka

Families who fled East Pakistan in 1971 have rebuilt their lives here. At first glance, Sindhanur seems more Bengal than Karnataka.

KM 13 · Mehsana, Gujarat · 2020

Rotis and a yoga class

In his first camp outside Delhi, Kiran challenges a young girl to teach him rotis. He can flip them with bare hands but drowns the dough, and the whole kitchen erupts in laughter. Soon after, the camp asks him to teach yoga to the children.

KM 14 · Near Radhanpur, Gujarat · 2020

The world's cheapest prince

The only way into the camp is a narrow canal bed, with thorns on one side and water on the other. Kiran refuses to leave the bike behind, and rides in to cheering children.

I feel like the world's cheapest prince: dusty bike, broken beard, sunburnt skin.

KM 15 · Radhanpur, Gujarat · December 2020

Thugs of water

Sixteen huts on a cracked field, five months without water. With no Indian papers, the men wait on the highway for work that rarely comes. At night the families walk to a dhaba to take water, until someone there quietly starts to help.

We steal water.

KM 16 · Jaisalmer, Rajasthan · On the Yatra

The sewing machine

Kiran finds Heera's family again in the desert and brings a box hidden under a cloth. Inside is a sewing machine, and a note.

Create, dream, and stitch your own world.

KM 17 · Jaisalmer, Rajasthan · On the Yatra

The first shirt

Before dawn on the day he leaves, Heera gives him a shirt she stitched through the night, using his old one for size. It fits perfectly. It becomes the first Stateless shirt, and today she leads its tailoring unit.

KM 18 · Arunachal Pradesh · On the Yatra

Citizens of nowhere

In the Northeast, the Chakma and Hajong communities have lived for five decades without the papers of any country.

Citizens of nowhere, trapped between borders and promises.

KM 19 · Meghalaya · On the Yatra

By the river

On a mountain road a bus overtakes on a blind curve and nearly takes him over the edge. A few kilometres later he finds the bluest, stillest river he has ever seen, and sits down beside it.

Alive. I smiled and kept smiling.

Four stories, one road

The four stories

01

One journey

A man leaves an ashram in Kerala and rides the length of India, living only on what strangers offer.

What would it take for you to just go and listen?

02

A border crossed

An eleven-year-old girl and her father walk into India with a parcel of visas and nothing else.

What would you carry if you could never go back?

03

Lives transformed

A sewing machine in a desert camp. A shirt stitched overnight. A tailoring unit that grew from it.

What changes a life: charity, or someone who stays?

04

A search for belonging

The man who stayed behind, the woman whose call began it all, and families who are still citizens of nowhere.

Where do you belong when no country claims you?

A question from the road

Who are your people?

Everyone Kiran met on the road answered this question differently: a family, a village, a faith, a stranger who shared water. We'd love to hear your answer. Selected stories will be shared on this page and on the wall at the IGNCA exhibition in New Delhi.

    Write a few lines, or record a short voice note, about the people you belong to.

  2. 02

    Add a photo if you like, and say how you'd like to be named.

  3. 03

    Send it. The public address will be published here.

A photo or a short voice note can travel with the letter once the address is published. Until then, copy it and keep it.

The rider

Kiran Chukkapalli

Kiran is an artist and humanitarian, founder of Think Peace and the Refugee Aid Project, and the creative force behind Stateless.

In March 2020 he went to the border at Attari to meet a family from Sindh as they crossed. An eleven-year-old girl called Heera asked to ride on the back of his motorcycle, and that morning set the course of the years that followed. In November 2020 he set out on a ride of more than 10,000 kilometres, staying with families in camps, desert shelters and forgotten colonies.

The work that grew from it has since reached more than 3,50,000 displaced people through twelve projects in ninety-two camps across seven states.
